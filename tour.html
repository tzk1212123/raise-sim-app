1 · What are we measuring?

Put a drop of liquid on a surface and look from the side. The angle between the drop and the surface is the contact angle. Big angle = round bead (water on a non-stick pan). Small angle = flat puddle.

We mix two soap-like ingredients into water. SDS makes the drop spread flat; Tween keeps it rounder. The goal: a drop of exactly 72°, using as little ingredient as possible.

2 · How does the robot test one recipe?

In the real lab a pipetting robot (Opentrons OT-2) does this. Every test costs a few minutes, consumables and is slightly noisy, which is why we can't test everything.

  1. 💧 Add water
  2. 🧴 Add SDS stock
  3. 🧴 Add Tween stock
  4. 🔄 Mix 10 times
  5. 🩸 Place 3 droplets
  6. 📷 Picks up camera, waits 10 s
  7. 🖼 Photograph & measure angles
  8. ✅ Report the result

3 · Why not just try everything?

Each ingredient comes in 15 or 30 allowed amounts: 450 recipes. At ~3.4 minutes each that is ~25 hours of robot time plus a person resetting the deck every 30 recipes. A good strategy finds a great recipe in 10–15 tests.

The catch: the strategy only learns what it tests. The map below is blank to it.

each square = one recipe · left→right more Tween · bottom→top more SDS

4 · The race: who finds a good recipe with the fewest tests?

Every contestant gets the same simulated lab, the same 3 random starter tests and the same budget. They only see their own measurements.

5 · Scoreboard: does the recommended recipe really work?

A lucky reading is not enough. Each contestant names one recipe, and we re-test it 5 times on a fresh setup. Then we compare it to the best recipe that exists.
